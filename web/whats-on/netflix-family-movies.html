Newly added — start here

Play tonight

Dog Man (2025)

PG · Added September 13 · Best for 6–10

The Dav Pilkey / DreamWorks movie finally hit the US Netflix kids row this week. Half-dog, half-cop, all slapstick. Your kid already knows the books; the film is the same energy — loud, goofy, slightly potty, never mean.

Watch for

  • Cartoon fights and explosions
  • Bathroom / dog-behavior gags
  • A villain who wants to be loved more than feared

Preview first

Puss in Boots: The Last Wish (2022)

PG · Added September 10 · Best for 8+

The best thing DreamWorks has made in years — and the one parents underestimate. The wolf is Death. He is persistent, quiet, and designed to scare. Fine for most 8–10s who already do Shrek; a hard pass for sensitive six-year-olds.

Watch for

  • A personification of Death who stalks Puss
  • Sword fights and a dark fairy-crime world
  • Panic-attack imagery when Puss hits his last life

Preschool pick

Monster at the End of This Story (2026)

TV-Y · Added September 7 · Best for 2–6

Sesame Street’s Grover-vs-the-last-page special. The whole joke is that the monster is him. Short, musical, and the only September Netflix add that is actually for toddlers.

Watch for

  • Mild pretend-scary buildup that resolves as a hug

Play tonight

Barbie in the Nutcracker (2001)

G · Added September 14 · Best for 4–8

The 2001 Clara-as-Barbie ballet movie, not a new theatrical. Mouse King, Sugarplum Fairy, no innuendo. A calm Sunday-afternoon watch if Dog Man is too shouty.

Watch for

  • A villain mouse army — cartoon, not nightmare fuel

New this week

Robots (2005)

PG · Added September 16 · Best for 6–10

Blue Sky’s scrapyard adventure landed Wednesday. Ewan McGregor, Robin Williams, a kid inventor who leaves home. Milder than Antz, funnier than the trailer suggested in 2005.

Watch for

  • Outdated-parts-as-euthanasia subtext if you squint
  • A few robot-body gags
  • Chase / chop sequences

Play tonight

Flushed Away (2006)

PG · Added September 1 · Best for 6+

Aardman rats in a London sewer. Toilet humor is the premise, not a side gag. The Toad is a cartoon tyrant; nothing here is meaner than a Saturday-morning chase.

Watch for

  • Poop / flush jokes throughout
  • Slapstick drowning scares

Preview first

Shark Tale (2004)

PG · Added September 1 · Best for 7+

Will Smith as a hustler fish in a mob parody. Fine as a silly underwater cartoon; the Godfather riffs and a few adult asides are why it isn’t the preschool pick.

Watch for

  • Mob-hit jokes (cartoon, not graphic)
  • Mild innuendo and booty gags
  • A character fakes another fish’s death for clout

Preview first

Antz (1998)

PG · Added September 1 · Best for 8+

Woody Allen’s neurotic worker ant vs. a genocidal general. The war termite battle is louder and darker than the insect-cute poster. Not A Bug’s Life.

Watch for

  • Insect-war carnage (stylized, still a battlefield)
  • A drowning / flood sequence
  • Adult one-liners aimed over kids’ heads

Preview first

The Prince of Egypt (1998)

PG · Added September 1 · Best for 8+

The one September DreamWorks title that is actually great — and not a cozy kids movie. Slavery, plagues, the death of the firstborn. Watch it with them if you want the conversation. Do not dump it on a five-year-old because it is animated.

Watch for

  • Enslavement and beatings
  • Plagues, including the death of children
  • A drowning army in the Red Sea

Preview first

The Flintstones (1994)

PG · Added September 1 · Best for 8+

Live-action John Goodman / Rick Moranis. Cartoon violence, workplace scheming, and 90s innuendo. A nostalgia play for you, not a default for little kids.

Watch for

  • Suggestive jokes
  • Smoking / drinking gags
  • A mistress subplot

Common questions

What is the best new family movie on Netflix right now?

As of September 18, 2026: Dog Man for elementary, Puss in Boots: The Last Wish for 8+, and Monster at the End of This Story for preschool.

Is Puss in Boots: The Last Wish too scary?

Often, yes, for under-8s. The wolf is a personification of Death. Older elementary kids who already watch Shrek usually handle it.

Are Antz and The Prince of Egypt okay for little kids because they’re animated?

No. Antz is a war movie with insects. The Prince of Egypt includes slavery and the death of firstborn children. Both are PG for a reason.
